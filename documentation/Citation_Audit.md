## Prioritized observations

1. **P2 — unsupported publication dates for three living documentation pages.** `thesis/references.md` labels the OpenAI documentation as 2026a/b/c, but the currently inspected pages expose no publication/update date establishing that year. An access date establishes retrieval, not publication. Use the institution's no-date convention and consistent disambiguation (for example, OpenAI, no date a/b/c) unless a dated source version is retained. Retain the genuine 22 September 2026 access date for the original consultation; this audit revisited them on 23 September. Official pages: [model](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-4.1-mini), [function calling](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling), [structured outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs). The [Open University Harvard guide](https://university.open.ac.uk/library/referencing-and-plagiarism/quick-guide-to-harvard-referencing-cite-them-right) uses “no date” where no publication date is given. It is a primary university style example, not a claim that Gisma mandates every detail of that variant.
2. **P2 — consistent final Harvard styling remains worthwhile.** The DOCX builder writes references as plain paragraphs: journal/proceedings/webpage titles are not italicized. The three NeurIPS entries identify venue, volume, year and a precise article URL but omit page spans/DOIs, whereas several other proceedings entries supply page spans. These are retrievable, legitimate references, not invented publications. Complete and style them consistently using the department's Harvard variant. There is no evidence that these minor formatting points invalidate the research. Publisher DOI links available on the article pages are: Self-Refine `10.52202/075280-2019`, Toolformer `10.52202/075280-2997`, ToolQA `10.52202/075280-2180`.
3. **Access limitations are not citation errors.** Direct OpenReview forum access returned browser challenges for Huang and AgentBench; their ICLR publisher pages and author manuscripts supplied matching metadata and claim support. Direct Euclid pages/PDFs rendered as an iframe in this tool, but publisher-indexed PDF text supplied the printed bibliographic headers and the relevant abstracts. The Kaggle live HTML exposed no body on direct open, but its indexed primary data card and the archived Kaggle JSON support the source description. The exact 2016-10-26 version date and Unknown licence were inspected in the saved primary metadata; the live API was not independently re-fetched successfully in this subtask. These limits should be retained in coverage claims.
4. **No demonstrated unattributed quotation problem.** The inspected literature descriptions are attributed paraphrases, with no long direct quotation presented as the student's own words. This does not establish a zero similarity score. A university originality report is a separate check, and common technical phrases or accurately attributed paraphrases are not automatically plagiarism.

## Claim calibration

The literature review is particularly careful about transfer: it does not call the local work a FActScore/G-Eval implementation, does not equate consistency with tau-bench pass^k, does not call prompting fine-tuning, and does not claim Huang proves all self-correction impossible. Local results are not compared numerically against incompatible published benchmarks. These are substantive strengths of the current citation use.

Possible strengthening for a higher-mark critical review would be a concise cross-paper synthesis of what evidence validators can and cannot establish, retaining the existing bounded novelty claim. It would be inappropriate to pad the bibliography with unrelated 2026 material solely to raise its count. The handbook's recency preference is already represented by 2025 HalluLens/DSBench and current official API documentation; foundational 2001/2003 statistical papers are appropriate for their role.
